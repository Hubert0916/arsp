\paragraph{Predictor effect with acquisition held fixed.}
Under Random acquisition, ASRP's mean ALC is $0.2008$ versus $0.2435$ for Empirical Mean, a reduction of $0.0426$. Under Adaptive acquisition, ASRP's mean ALC is $0.1987$ versus $0.2403$ for Empirical Mean, a reduction of $0.0416$. Empirical Mean + Random rises from $0.2500$ at zero labels to $0.3488$ at one label: a single outcome makes its prediction either zero or one. ASRP smooths early evidence using public priors. At budget zero the acquisition policies have no effect. The Rasch hybrid followed by topic correction supplies the initial prediction; the kernel branch receives increasing weight as labels become available.

\paragraph{Acquisition effect with the predictor held fixed.}
For ASRP, Adaptive lowers mean ALC by $0.0021$ relative to Random, and has lower ALC in all three seeds. For Empirical Mean, Adaptive lowers mean ALC by $0.0031$ relative to Random, and has lower ALC in 2 of the three seeds. The closest ASRP pair differs by only $-0.00005$ (Adaptive minus Random, seed 43). Table~\ref{tab:seeds} shows the individual seed results and their sample standard deviation; three seeds summarize sensitivity to these draws rather than establishing universal superiority.

\paragraph{Groups with more room for selection.}
The prespecified subset of 78 groups with at least 160 items leaves at least 80 acquisition candidates (Table~\ref{tab:large}). In this subset, Adaptive lowers ASRP's mean ALC by $0.0026$ relative to Random. In this subset, Adaptive lowers Empirical Mean's mean ALC by $0.0023$ relative to Random. Across the full experiment, 273 of 276 Adaptive trajectories execute at least one discretionary decision. The 3 trajectories without discretionary choices belong to one 90-item group: 14 warm-up skips leave exactly 31 candidates, all mandatory. Topic calibration is active; the source-name guard remains inactive because the benchmark aliases are anonymous.
