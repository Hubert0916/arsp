\paragraph{Predictor effect with acquisition held fixed.}
Relative to Empirical Mean with matched labels, ASRP reduces ALC by $0.0427$ under Random and $0.0418$ under Adaptive acquisition. Empirical Mean + Random rises from $0.2500$ at zero labels to $0.3488$ at one label: a single outcome makes its prediction either zero or one. ASRP smooths early evidence using public priors. This comparison measures the complete predictor, including its frozen public-data information; component removals are examined in Section~\ref{sec:ablations}.

\paragraph{Acquisition effect with the predictor held fixed.}
For ASRP, Adaptive lowers mean ALC by $0.0022$ relative to Random, and has lower ALC in all three seeds. For Empirical Mean, Adaptive lowers mean ALC by $0.0031$ relative to Random, and has lower ALC in 2 of the three seeds. The closest ASRP pair differs by only $-0.00057$ (Adaptive minus Random, seed 43). Table~\ref{tab:seeds} shows the individual seed results and their sample standard deviation; three seeds summarize sensitivity to these draws rather than establishing universal superiority.

\paragraph{Variation across sources.}
Averaging the paired ALC differences over seeds within each source, ASRP beats Empirical Mean on 83 of 92 sources under Adaptive acquisition and loses on 9. For ASRP, Adaptive improves on Random in 56 sources, worsens in 33, and ties in 3 (tolerance $10^{-12}$). These counts describe the selected subject per source.
