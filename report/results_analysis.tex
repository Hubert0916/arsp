\paragraph{Predictor effect with acquisition held fixed.}
Under Random acquisition, ASRP's mean ALC is $0.2008$ versus $0.2435$ for Empirical Mean, a reduction of $0.0426$. Under Adaptive acquisition, ASRP's mean ALC is $0.1987$ versus $0.2403$ for Empirical Mean, a reduction of $0.0416$. At budget zero the acquisition policies have no effect. The Rasch hybrid supplies the initial prediction; the kernel branch receives increasing weight as labels become available.

\paragraph{Acquisition effect with the predictor held fixed.}
For ASRP, Adaptive lowers mean ALC by $0.0021$ relative to Random, and has lower ALC in all three seeds. For Empirical Mean, Adaptive lowers mean ALC by $0.0031$ relative to Random, and has lower ALC in 2 of the three seeds. Table~\ref{tab:seeds} shows the individual seed results and their sample standard deviation; three seeds summarize sensitivity to these draws rather than establishing universal superiority.
