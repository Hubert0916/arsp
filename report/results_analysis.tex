\paragraph{Predictor effect with acquisition held fixed.}
Under Random acquisition, ASRP's mean ALC is $0.2008$ versus $0.2435$ for Empirical Mean, a reduction of $0.0426$. Under Adaptive acquisition, ASRP's mean ALC is $0.1987$ versus $0.2403$ for Empirical Mean, a reduction of $0.0416$. Empirical Mean + Random rises from $0.2500$ at zero labels to $0.3488$ at one label: a single outcome makes its prediction either zero or one. ASRP smooths early evidence using public priors. This comparison measures the complete predictor, including its frozen public-data information. At budget zero, the hybrid combines metadata smoothing and Rasch predictions before topic correction; the kernel weight is zero. At budgets 15 and 31, the kernel weight is one.

\paragraph{Acquisition effect with the predictor held fixed.}
For ASRP, Adaptive lowers mean ALC by $0.0021$ relative to Random, and has lower ALC in all three seeds. For Empirical Mean, Adaptive lowers mean ALC by $0.0031$ relative to Random, and has lower ALC in 2 of the three seeds. The closest ASRP pair differs by only $-0.00005$ (Adaptive minus Random, seed 43). Table~\ref{tab:seeds} shows the individual seed results and their sample standard deviation; three seeds summarize sensitivity to these draws rather than establishing universal superiority.

\paragraph{Variation across sources.}
Averaging the paired ALC differences over seeds within each source, ASRP beats Empirical Mean on 82 of 92 sources under Adaptive acquisition and loses on 10. For ASRP, Adaptive improves on Random in 60 sources, worsens in 31, and ties in 1 (tolerance $10^{-12}$). These counts describe the selected subject per source; the aggregate gain does not imply a gain on every source.

\paragraph{Selection capacity.}
Across the full experiment, 273 of 276 Adaptive trajectories execute at least one discretionary decision. The 3 trajectories without discretionary choices belong to one 90-item group: 14 warm-up skips leave exactly 31 candidates, all mandatory.
